From song to beat-synced rough cut

Beatline listens to the song first, then plans the cuts, then hands Final Cut Pro a project that is already timed to the music.

0. Start Beatline

In Terminal, in the Beatline folder, run ./beatline.command (or double-click it in Finder). It opens Beatline in its own window and, unlike a browser tab, can find your song on disk so Final Cut links it correctly.

1. Listen and check

  1. Drop the song anywhere in the window. Beatline works out the tempo, beats, bar starts, time signature, sections, key and loudness in a couple of seconds.
  2. Read “What Beatline heard”. The confidence badge tells you how sure it is. Unsure means the song has no steady pulse (speech, rubato, free time): cut on Drums or Any hit instead, or tap the tempo.
  3. Listen with the click. Press C (or Click) and Space. The click plays on every cut, accented on bar starts.
    • Feels twice or half as fast? Click a tempo chip (½×, 2×, ⅔×, 1.5×) under the tempo.
    • Bar numbers start on the wrong beat? Put the playhead on the real “one” and press D.
    • Whole grid a hair early or late? [ and ] nudge it by 10 ms.
    • Live band drifting? Beatline says so under the tempo; follow the Loosen the grid link.
The timeline shows, top to bottom: bar numbers, the song’s sections (calm, groove, peak, drop), the tempo over time, and the waveform coloured by bass (blue), mids (teal) and highs (white). Faint ticks mark every beat; strong lines are your cuts.

2. Plan the cuts

  1. Choose what to cut on (Cuts tab):
    Edit styleSetting
    Music video, event recapBeat grid · Every bar
    Fast montage, sports, hypeBeat grid · Every beat or 2nd beat
    Wedding film, slow emotionalBeat grid · Every 2 or 4 bars
    Builds to a dropBeat grid · Auto: follow the song’s energy
    Punchy accentsDrums · Kick and/or Snare
    No steady beatAny hit
  2. Check the numbers. The cut plan shows total cuts, shots to fill, average shot length and cuts per minute, and the same per section. That’s how many clips you need to pull.
  3. Set a range for cut-downs. Drag across the ruler, press I/O, or use the 6/15/30/60/90 s buttons (they start at the playhead and end on a bar line).
  4. Fine-tune. Shortest shot drops cuts that would make flash frames (shown faded). Cut early moves every cut 1–2 frames ahead of the beat, which often reads tighter. Shift+click adds or removes a single cut; M adds one at the playhead.

3. Export to Final Cut

  1. Pick the export (Export tab): Song + markers, Pre-cut (a placeholder per shot, already timed) or Markers only.
  2. Check “Song location” shows ✓ Found. Final Cut links the song by its full path; a wrong path imports a silent, offline clip. Beatline fills this in for you when started with beatline.command.
  3. Set the frame rate to your Final Cut project’s, then Export to Final Cut (⌘E). Double-click the downloaded .fcpxml, or in Final Cut choose File › Import › XML….

4. Cut in Final Cut Pro

  • Pre-cut timeline: drag a clip from the browser onto a placeholder and choose Replace from Start. It takes the placeholder’s exact length, so every cut stays on the beat. Work left to right.
  • Song + markers: select the song and Edit › Lift from Storyline (⌥⌘↑), turn on snapping (N), then trim or overwrite to the markers. ⌃' / ⌃; jump marker to marker.
  • The Timeline Index (⇧⌘2 › Tags) lists every marker by name: sections, “Bar 12”, “Snare 40”.
