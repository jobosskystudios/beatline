Cut a rough cut to the beat in Final Cut Pro

From song to a beat-synced rough cut in about ten minutes: mark the music in Beatline, import the markers, then let snapping do the timing.

1. Mark the song

  1. Keep the song where it will stay. The exported file points at the song’s location on your Mac, so move it into your project folder before exporting.
  2. Drop the song into Beatline. It finds the tempo and beat grid in a second or two.
  3. Check by ear. Turn on 🔔 Click and press Space. The click should land on the kick/snare.
    • Click twice as fast or half as fast as the song feels? Use ½× or 2×.
    • Bar numbers start on the wrong beat? Move the downbeat with ◀ ▶.
    • Live band or drifting tempo? Lower Grid strictness.
    • Markers feel a hair late? Use Nudge (e.g. −20 ms) or Snap to frame: Previous.
  4. Pick a density for the cut you want.
    Style of editMarkers
    Fast montage, sports, hypeEvery beat or every 2nd beat
    Music video, event recapEvery bar
    Slow, emotional, wedding filmEvery 2 or 4 bars
    Accent cuts on drum hitsMode: Hits
  5. Edit by hand where needed. Shift+click the waveform to add or remove a marker; press M while playing to tap one in. Your markers export as red to-do markers so you can spot them.
  6. Export. Set the frame rate to match your FCP project, choose Song + markers, paste the song’s folder path and click Download .fcpxml.
Getting the folder path: in Finder select the folder, hold ⌥ Option, right-click, and choose Copy “…” as Pathname. Paste it into Beatline — it’s remembered for next time.

2. Bring it into Final Cut Pro

  1. File › Import › XML… and choose the downloaded .fcpxml (or just drag it onto the FCP window).
  2. Pick your library. Beatline creates an event (default Beatline) with a project named “Song – beats”. Open it: the song sits in the timeline with every marker on it.
  3. If the clip shows as missing media, select it and use File › Relink Files… to point at the song.
  4. Make the song a connected clip so video can go in the primary storyline: select it and choose Edit › Lift from Storyline (⌥⌘↑). The markers move with it.

3. Cut to the markers

  1. Turn snapping on (N). Playhead, skimmer, edits and range selections now snap to markers.
  2. Jump marker to marker with ⌃' (next) and ⌃; (previous).
  3. Fill each beat gap: put the playhead on a marker and press I, jump to the next marker and press O. Then mark the start of a shot in the browser (I) and press D (Overwrite) — FCP fills exactly the marked range in the timeline.
  4. Faster rough version: lay down your selects end to end, then trim each edit point to the nearest marker — with snapping on they click into place.
  5. Open the Timeline Index (⇧⌘2 → Tags) to see and jump to every marker by name (“Bar 12”, “Bar 12 · Beat 3”…).

Markers-only export

Prefer not to type a folder path? Choose Markers only. The project then contains a gap clip carrying the markers. Drag your song under it as a connected clip starting at 0, and snap your edits to the gap’s markers.
