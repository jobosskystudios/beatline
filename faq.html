FAQ

The project opens in Final Cut but the song is silent.

Final Cut found the project but not the audio file, so the clip is offline (often red, no waveform). The FCPXML points at the song by its full path, and a browser can’t see where a file lives. Fixes:

  • Start Beatline with ./beatline.command. It finds the song on your Mac and shows ✓ Found in the Export tab before you export.
  • Or type the folder yourself. Beatline refuses to export when it can confirm the folder is wrong.
  • Already imported? Select the clip in Final Cut and choose File › Relink Files….
  • Don’t move or rename the song after exporting.

The markers looked random on my song.

The first version could lock onto the wrong tempo (for example 3/4 of the real one), and then the grid drifts in and out of the music. The current engine estimates tempo over many windows, checks half and double time, and shows a confidence badge. If it still looks off: try the tempo chips, press D on the real “one”, or tell us which song so we can add it to the test set.

Final Cut Pro 12 has beat detection built in. Why use this?

Use whichever works for the song. What Beatline adds, based on what editors report about the built-in grid:

  • You can fix it. Tempo chips, bar 1 at the playhead, grid nudge, strictness for live bands. Final Cut’s grid can’t be adjusted.
  • It tells you when it’s unsure, instead of drawing a confident-looking wrong grid.
  • Not just 4/4. Waltzes (3/4) are detected automatically, and 5, 6 or 7 beats per bar can be set by hand; Apple’s guide recommends 4/4 material.
  • It plans the edit: cut counts, shot list, energy-aware pacing, cut-downs, and a pre-cut timeline, not only grid lines.
  • Drum markers: cut on kicks or snares, not only the beat.
  • Works with older Final Cut versions and exports CSV for other editors.

Some editors reported that the built-in markers in Final Cut 12.2 were further off than in 12.0 on the same songs. Beatline’s results don’t depend on Final Cut’s version.

The song speeds up or slows down.

Press Find best-fitting grid (Grid tab), lower the strictness, or fix it by hand: drag a beat line, and hold ⌥ to move every later beat with it.

Is there a neural-network (“AI”) beat tracker or stem separation?

Not yet. Both need large trained models that Beatline doesn’t ship. The Find best-fitting grid search is the high-accuracy option today; it runs automatically when Beatline is unsure.

The tempo is double (or half) what I feel.

Very common with half-time grooves. Click a tempo chip under the tempo, or type the BPM you want in the Beat grid tab.

The cuts feel slightly late.

Set Cut early to 1 or 2 frames (Cuts tab). Video often feels tighter when the cut lands just before the hit. To shift the beat grid itself, use [ and ].

Which frame rate should I choose?

Your Final Cut project’s rate (Modify › Project Properties). Cuts are snapped to that rate’s frames.

How is “total cuts” counted?

Every cut inside your range that survives the shortest-shot limit. Shots = cuts + 1.

The sections look wrong.

Sections come from loudness only, so a quiet chorus can read as “calm”. Use them as a pacing guide.

Is my music uploaded anywhere?

No. Decoding, analysis and export happen on your Mac. The launcher’s file search runs locally and only answers Beatline itself.

Can I use the markers in Premiere Pro or DaVinci Resolve?

Yes: Premiere XML (a sequence with the song and markers) and Resolve EDL (timeline markers) in the Export tab, plus marker and shot lists as CSV.

Which clips work for “My clips”?

MOV and MP4 files (ProRes, H.264, HEVC). Beatline reads each clip’s length, frame rate and size from the file itself. Shots longer than every clip stay as placeholders.

Can I use it offline, as an app?

Yes. Run ./tools/make-mac-app.sh once to get Beatline.app in ~/Applications, or install it from Chrome/Edge (Install Beatline) or Safari (File › Add to Dock).
